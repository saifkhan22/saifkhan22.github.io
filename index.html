🧭 Your First Calculation, Step by Step

A load calculation sounds intimidating until you break it into small pieces. Here is the order most people follow, and the order the skill expects.

  1. Describe the building. Note the floor area, number of storeys, and the general shape of the house.
  2. List the surfaces. Walls, roof, floor over a crawlspace, windows, and doors. For each one, note roughly how big it is.
  3. Note the construction. Brick, timber frame, insulation levels, single or double glazing. Approximate answers are fine for a first pass.
  4. Pick the design conditions. These are the outdoor temperatures for the coldest and hottest days in your area. Your local weather data will have them.
  5. Set the indoor temperature. Most homes are designed for around 21 degrees Celsius in winter and 24 in summer.
  6. Run the skill. It combines the surfaces, the insulation, and the temperature difference into a heat loss figure.
  7. Read the result. You get a heating load and a cooling load. Match those numbers against equipment capacity when you shop for a heat pump.

📚 Glossary for Beginners

  • Heating load: the amount of heat a building loses on a cold day, measured as the power needed to replace it.
  • Cooling load: the amount of heat a building gains on a hot day.
  • Heat loss: warmth escaping through walls, windows, roofs, and floors.
  • Heat pump: a device that moves heat rather than generating it, used for both heating and cooling.
  • HPXML: a shared file format for describing homes and their energy features.
  • EnergyPlus: a well-known simulation engine used to model building energy use.
  • OpenStudio: a friendly interface for building and running energy models.
